# Recipe 0002 — Homemade Chicken Soup

**Status:** Draft — kitchen validation pending  
**Adaptation:** Instant Pot + Cuisinart  
**Source:** User-supplied photograph of the "Homemade Chicken Soup" recipe in *Breaking the Vicious Cycle*; Curadh Cooking method below is an original adaptation of the recipe facts rather than a transcription.

## Goal

A simple chicken-and-carrot soup with a full chicken broth, a smooth carrot-thickened body, and optional aromatics.

The source recipe uses chicken parts, carrots, onion, celery, parsley, salt, and water; simmers for about four hours; strains and skims the broth; then purees the carrots back into the soup.

This version preserves that structure while using an Instant Pot for the cook and a Cuisinart blender/food processor for the carrot puree.

## Yield

About **6 bowls**, depending on final water level and how much chicken is returned to the soup.

## Ingredients

- 2 1/2 to 3 lb bone-in chicken thighs and/or drumsticks
- 6 to 8 medium carrots, peeled and cut into large pieces
- 1 large yellow onion, quartered — optional if the selected profile flags it
- 2 to 3 celery stalks, cut in half — optional if the selected profile flags it
- 8 to 10 parsley sprigs — optional if the selected profile flags it
- 4 to 6 cups cold water, using only enough to nearly cover the ingredients while remaining below the applicable pressure-cook fill line for the Instant Pot
- 2 tsp fine salt to start, plus more at the end if wanted

### Profile-specific option

If onion, celery, or parsley is individually flagged by the selected profile, leave that ingredient out rather than treating it as a universal MCAS rule.

## Tools

- Instant Pot or comparable electric pressure cooker
- colander or fine-mesh strainer
- large bowl or second pot
- Cuisinart blender or food processor
- ladle
- instant-read food thermometer
- shallow storage containers for leftovers

## Instant Pot setup

- **Program:** Pressure Cook / Manual
- **Pressure:** High
- **Cook time:** 20 minutes
- **Release:** 15 minutes natural release, then carefully release the remaining pressure
- **Keep Warm:** Off for the validation run if practical

Do not exceed the fill limit that applies to pressure cooking/soup on the specific Instant Pot model.

## Method

1. Put the chicken, carrots, onion, celery, parsley, and 2 tsp salt into the Instant Pot.
2. Add 4 to 6 cups cold water, using only enough to nearly cover the ingredients without exceeding the applicable pressure-cook fill line.
3. Lock the lid and cook on **High Pressure for 20 minutes**.
4. When the cook ends, allow **15 minutes of natural pressure release**, then carefully release the remaining pressure according to the Instant Pot instructions.
5. Check the thickest chicken piece with an instant-read thermometer. Poultry must reach **165°F / 74°C**. If it has not, cook it further before serving.
6. Lift out the chicken and set it aside.
7. Strain the broth through a colander or fine-mesh strainer into a large bowl or second pot. Keep the cooked carrots. Discard the spent parsley, celery, and onion unless there is a specific reason to retain them.
8. Skim excess surface fat from the broth if desired. Do not worry about removing every trace.
9. Put the cooked carrots in the Cuisinart with **1 cup of the strained broth**. Blend until smooth. Add another 1/2 to 1 cup broth only if needed to make the puree move cleanly.
10. Stir the carrot puree back into the remaining broth.
11. Remove bones, skin, and cartilage from the chicken. Return as much shredded chicken to the soup as desired.
12. Taste the finished soup and adjust salt. For a profile with a clinician-set higher sodium target, adjust the finished pot or individual bowl to that target rather than treating this recipe's salt amount as a universal POTS dose.
13. Serve hot.

## Expected cues for the first validation run

- Broth tastes distinctly chicken-forward rather than watery.
- Carrot puree gives the broth a lightly velvety body without turning it into carrot puree soup.
- Chicken is very tender and easy to pull from the bone.
- Salt is noticeable but still adjustable at the bowl.
- Onion/celery/parsley support the broth rather than dominate it.

## POTS fact-check

POTS guidance commonly includes increased oral fluid and sodium for appropriate patients. Expert reviews cite roughly 2 to 3 L/day of fluid and increased salt/sodium as common non-pharmacologic management, while also noting that targets should be individualized and may be inappropriate with conditions such as hypertension or other contraindications.

For this recipe:

- soup can be a practical **fluid + sodium vehicle**;
- the base recipe should not pretend one sodium amount is medically correct for every person;
- final salt is deliberately adjustable so a personal/clinician-set sodium target can override the recipe default.

## MCAS / histamine fact-check

There is **not one evidence-based universal MCAS food-avoid list**. Recent mast-cell guidance cautions against indiscriminate food elimination; individual triggers matter.

For this recipe:

- chicken, carrot, onion, celery, and parsley should be evaluated against the person's actual profile rather than automatically labeled safe or unsafe;
- freshness and storage can matter because biogenic amines in meat can increase during storage;
- histamine already present in food is heat-stable, so pressure cooking should **not** be described as destroying histamine or making an ingredient universally MCAS-safe;
- the Instant Pot adaptation is for convenience and reproducibility, not a claim of histamine removal;
- promptly chill leftovers. For a person whose profile is sensitive to stored meats, freezing portions that will not be eaten promptly is a reasonable profile-specific handling strategy.

Sources reviewed:

- Histamine heat stability review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8308327/

## Food-safety handling

- Use a thermometer; chicken should reach **165°F / 74°C**.
- Refrigerate or freeze cooked leftovers within **2 hours**.
- Use shallow containers for faster cooling.

Source reviewed:

- CDC chicken food safety: https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/chicken.html
