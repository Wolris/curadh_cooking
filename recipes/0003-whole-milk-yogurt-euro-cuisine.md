# Recipe 0003 — Whole-Milk Yogurt — Euro Cuisine

**Status:** Draft — first kitchen validation in progress  
**Variant:** V1 — whole milk + Yogourmet Original + gelatin  
**Equipment target:** Euro Cuisine 7-jar yogurt maker  
**Source basis:** Yogourmet Original starter directions + Yogourmet firmer-yogurt guidance + Euro Cuisine 7-jar maker guidance. Curadh Cooking method below adapts those facts into the actual 7-jar household workflow.

## Goal

A plain, thick whole-milk yogurt made in a Euro Cuisine 7-jar yogurt maker, using Yogourmet Original starter and a small amount of unflavored gelatin for a firmer refrigerated set.

This is intentionally a simple baseline batch. Flavoring, fruit, sweetener, and strained/Greek-style variants should come later so the first run can establish whether the culture, texture, tang, and machine timing work reliably.

## Yield

About **4 cups / 32 fl oz / 946 mL total**, divided among **7 partially filled 6-oz jars**.

## Ingredients

- 4 cups whole milk
- 1 sachet (**3 g**) Yogourmet Original yogurt starter
- **3.5 g unflavored gelatin** — approximately half of a 7 g packet

### Why the gelatin is here

Yogourmet's firmer-yogurt guidance uses **7 g gelatin for 2 L milk** and says to use half that amount for 1 L. This V1 uses 3.5 g with a roughly 1 L / 1 quart batch.

Gelatin is a texture ingredient. It does **not** replace the live yogurt starter.

## Tools

- Euro Cuisine 7-jar yogurt maker
- 7 clean glass yogurt jars
- medium saucepan
- measuring cup
- spoon or whisk
- small cup or bowl for dissolving starter
- heat-safe bowl, optional, for faster cooling
- larger bowl for a cold-water bath, optional
- food thermometer, optional but preferred

## Time

- Active prep: about **15 to 25 minutes**
- Incubation: **minimum 5 hours; target 5 to 8 hours for the first run**
- Refrigeration/set: about **8 hours**
- Total elapsed time: roughly **13 to 17 hours**

## Method

1. **Clean and dry the jars and utensils.** Keep the individual jar lids off for incubation.

2. **Mix in the gelatin while the milk is cold.** Put about 1/4 to 1/2 cup of the cold milk in a small cup, stir in 3.5 g unflavored gelatin, then combine that mixture with the remaining cold milk in the saucepan.

3. **Heat the milk + gelatin.** Heat over medium stovetop heat, stirring the bottom regularly so the milk does not scorch.
   - Preferred measured target: **180°F / 82°C**.
   - Yogourmet also permits bringing the milk to a boil.
   - No-thermometer cue: watch closely once the milk is strongly steaming and beginning to swell/foam toward a boil. Remove it promptly rather than letting it boil over.
   - Do not use a fixed number of minutes as the primary doneness test; pan, burner, starting temperature, and volume change the timing.

4. **Cool before adding starter.** Cool the milk to **108–112°F / 42–44°C**.
   - No-thermometer cue from Yogourmet: with clean hands, the milk should feel **warm but not hot** to a finger.
   - To speed cooling, set the saucepan or a heat-safe bowl into a larger bowl containing cold water and stir periodically.
   - If using glass, avoid abrupt thermal shock from very hot glass directly into ice water unless the vessel is specifically designed for it.
   - Do not add the starter while the milk is still genuinely hot.

5. **Add the live culture.** Put about 1/2 cup of the cooled warm milk in a clean cup. Add the full **3 g Yogourmet Original sachet** and stir until well dispersed. Stir that mixture gently back into the remaining milk.

6. **Fill the jars.** Divide the cultured milk among the 7 Euro Cuisine jars. They will not be completely full.

7. **Incubate with the small lids OFF.** Put the uncovered jars into the yogurt maker and place the machine's large clear cover over them.

8. **Incubate.** Run the yogurt maker for at least **5 hours**. For this first Curadh Cooking run, use **5 to 8 hours** as the working window.
   - Shorter incubation generally gives a milder result.
   - Longer incubation generally increases acidity/tang and may increase firmness.
   - Do not keep disturbing or stirring the jars while they incubate.

9. **Chill immediately after incubation.** Turn off the machine, put the individual lids on the jars, and move them directly to the refrigerator.

10. **Let the yogurt set cold.** Refrigerate for about **8 hours** before judging the final texture. The gelatin develops much of its firmness during chilling, so the warm yogurt is not the final texture.

11. **Keep refrigerated and use promptly.** Yogourmet recommends consuming the finished yogurt within **7 days**.

## Expected cues for the first validation run

- The yogurt has a clear set after refrigeration rather than remaining milk-thin.
- Texture is creamy rather than rubbery or gelatin-dessert-like.
- Gelatin supports firmness without dominating the mouthfeel.
- Tang is pleasant and not excessively sharp.
- Minimal whey separation is acceptable; substantial separation should be recorded.
- No unusual color, mold, or putrid/off odor is present.

## Profile-fit notes

### POTS-oriented profiles

This recipe is primarily a cultured dairy food, **not** a deliberately high-sodium recipe. Whether it fits a POTS-oriented profile depends on that person's nutrition goals, dairy tolerance, and other explicit profile settings rather than the recipe receiving a universal POTS label.

### MCAS / low-histamine-oriented profiles

Yogurt is a fermented food. Fermented foods are commonly flagged in some low-histamine approaches, but published low-histamine food lists are inconsistent and fermented-food biogenic-amine content can vary with raw material, cultures, processing, and storage.

For Curadh Cooking:

- do not label this yogurt universally safe or unsafe for MCAS;
- surface fermentation as a calm profile-aware advisory when the selected mapping/profile calls for it;
- explicit ingredient-level acceptance or avoidance remains authoritative;
- record personal tolerance only as Cook Run evidence, not as a universal medical claim.
