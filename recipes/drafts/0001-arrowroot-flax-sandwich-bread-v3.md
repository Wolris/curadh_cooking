# Recipe 0001 — Arrowroot/Flax Sandwich Bread — Reconstruction V3

**Status:** Draft — awaiting kitchen validation

## Recovered history

- GF V1 was denser, used more eggs, and did not use cassava.
- GF V2 was less dense and used yeast.
- The Breadman handled the rise during its program.
- This reconstruction is therefore V3.

## Target

Sandwich/toast loaf; soft/fluffy; between moist and dry, leaning dry; flexible rather than crumbly; lighter than V2 if possible; neutral/savory; soft crust.

## V3 formula

All measurements use standard US cups/spoons.

### Dry

- 1 cup finely ground oat flour
- 3/4 cup arrowroot starch/flour
- 1/2 cup cassava flour
- 6 Tbsp cornstarch
- 3 Tbsp ground flaxseed meal
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1 tsp fine salt
- 2 tsp active dry yeast

### Wet

- 2 large eggs
- 3/4 cup + 2 Tbsp milk
- 1 Tbsp + 1 tsp honey
- 2 Tbsp olive oil
- 2 tsp apple cider vinegar

## Why this changed

Yeast is restored as the primary lift because it was already present in the less-dense V2. Baking powder is reduced to a small supporting amount. Baking soda is removed.

The extra egg white from the earlier reconstruction is removed because the denser historical V1 used more eggs. This does not prove eggs caused the density difference, but there is no reason to move back toward that direction without evidence.

Cassava remains moderate. The historical comparison does not support blaming cassava for density.

Almond and tigernut remain deferred for this test.

## Oat flour

If using rolled oats, grind very finely in the blender, then measure 1 cup of the finished oat flour.

## Breadman cycle

Use the same yeast-capable Breadman cycle used for V2 if remembered.

If the prior cycle is not remembered:

1. prefer the machine's Gluten-Free cycle if available;
2. do not use Quick Bread/Cake for V3;
3. if V2 worked on Basic/White and there is no Gluten-Free cycle, use that same cycle for comparability.

Record the exact cycle used.

## Method

1. Bring eggs and milk reasonably close to room temperature.
2. Add milk, eggs, honey, olive oil, and vinegar to the Breadman pan.
3. Whisk oat flour, arrowroot, cassava, cornstarch, flax, baking powder, and salt together thoroughly.
4. Add the dry blend over the wet ingredients.
5. Add active dry yeast according to the Breadman's normal yeast-loading convention.
6. Start the selected yeast-capable cycle.
7. During initial mixing, scrape the sides/corners once if needed.

The mixed dough should be a thick cohesive batter/dough that mounds softly and is spreadable, not pourable and not a conventional wheat dough ball.

- Too stiff/dry: add milk 1 Tbsp at a time.
- Too loose/pourable: add oat flour 1 Tbsp at a time.

Let the machine finish its rise and bake program.

Remove promptly after baking. For a softer crust, brush the hot top lightly with butter or olive oil and cool loosely under a clean towel.

Cool completely before slicing, ideally at least 90 minutes.

## Doneness

The center should be set with no wet/gummy line. If checked with an instant-read thermometer, roughly 200–205°F is a useful center target.

## Kitchen validation

After cooling, report:

1. lift/density compared with remembered V1 and V2;
2. crumb/flexibility;
3. moisture/gumminess;
4. sandwich and toast performance;
5. flavor and crust;
6. exact Breadman cycle and visible rise/collapse behavior;
7. any extra milk/oat flour used;
8. approximate loaf height/shape;
9. next-day slicing/flexibility if available.

## Interpretation rules

- Good rise but still dense: tune flour/starch balance before adding more leavener.
- Rise then collapse: investigate structure, hydration, and machine cycle before increasing yeast.
- Light but fragile: strengthen binding modestly.
- Gummy: evaluate hydration, cassava proportion, and bake completion.
- Dry/crumbly: modestly increase hydration, fat, or binding.

Do not attribute V1/V2 density differences to one ingredient alone; several variables changed.
