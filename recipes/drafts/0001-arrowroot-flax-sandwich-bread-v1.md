# Recipe 0001 — Arrowroot/Flax Sandwich Bread — Draft V1

**Status:** Draft — awaiting kitchen validation  
**Goal:** A wheat-free sandwich/toast loaf with a soft crust, neutral flavor, flexible slices, and a substantially lighter/drier crumb than the prior dense/moist versions.

## Target

- sandwich + toast use;
- soft/fluffy relative to prior gluten-free versions;
- between moist and dry, leaning dry;
- flexible rather than crumbly;
- not dense;
- mostly neutral/savory;
- soft crust;
- quick-bread method in a Breadman/bread machine.

## Why this version

V1 keeps the remembered arrowroot + flax + cassava + egg + honey family, but deliberately reduces the ingredients most likely to push the loaf toward heaviness.

Oat flour is introduced as the main new bread-like flour. Arrowroot and cornstarch provide a lighter starch fraction. Cassava is kept moderate because too much can produce a dense/gummy crumb. Flax is kept low enough to bind without making the loaf heavy or strongly flavored.

Almond flour and tigernut flour are intentionally excluded from V1. They remain future variants if needed.

Yeast is also excluded from V1. This version first reconstructs the quick-bread path rather than introducing a proofing process and a second major variable at the same time.

## Ingredients

### Dry

| Ingredient | Weight | Approx. volume | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| finely ground oat flour | 110 g | about 1 cup | bread-like body, softness |
| arrowroot starch/flour | 95 g | about 3/4 cup | lightness, flexibility |
| cassava flour | 75 g | about 1/2 cup | body and slice structure |
| cornstarch | 50 g | about 6 Tbsp | lighter crumb |

### Wet

| Ingredient | Weight | Approx. volume | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---|

| milk | 190 g | about 3/4 cup + 1 Tbsp | hydration |
| honey | 28–30 g | about 1 1/3 Tbsp | slight sweetness, browning, tenderness |

## Oat flour

If using rolled oats, process in the blender until very fine, then measure **110 g after grinding**.

If strict gluten avoidance becomes relevant for a profile, oat sourcing/cross-contact must be treated separately from the recipe formula rather than assumed from the word "oat."

## Breadman method

1. Bring eggs and milk reasonably close to room temperature.
2. Add the wet ingredients to the bread-machine pan: milk, eggs/egg white, honey, olive oil, and vinegar.
3. In a separate bowl, whisk the oat flour, arrowroot, cassava, cornstarch, flax, baking powder, baking soda, and salt very thoroughly.
4. Add the dry mixture evenly over the wet ingredients.
5. Use the machine's **Quick Bread / Cake / non-yeast quick-bread cycle**, preferably with a light-crust setting if the machine exposes one.
6. During the first mixing phase, scrape the corners/sides once so no dry flour pockets remain.
7. Judge the batter after it is fully mixed. It should be a **very thick batter**, closer to soft mashed potatoes or thick muffin batter than kneadable bread dough.
   - If it is stiff enough to sit in a dry lump, add milk **1 Tbsp / 15 g at a time**, mixing fully before adding more.
   - If it flows like pancake batter, add **1 Tbsp oat flour at a time**.
8. Let the cycle bake fully. Avoid opening the lid repeatedly during the baking portion.
9. Doneness target: center should be set with no wet/gummy line. If using an instant-read thermometer, a useful target is roughly **200–205°F / 93–96°C** in the center.
10. Remove the loaf from the pan promptly after baking.
11. For a softer crust, brush the hot top lightly with butter or olive oil and cool under a clean towel.
12. **Cool completely before slicing** — ideally at least 90 minutes. Do not judge crumb moisture while hot.

## Expected first-test result

This should be lighter and less gummy than a cassava-heavy/flax-heavy loaf, but it may still be somewhat more substantial than conventional white wheat bread.

The intended first-test crumb is:

- small-to-medium even air cells;
- soft but not wet;
- a 3/8–1/2 inch slice can bend without immediately splitting;
- sturdy enough for sandwich use;
- able to toast without crumbling apart;
- neutral enough that honey/flax do not dominate.

## Intentionally deferred substitutions

Do not change these on the first bake unless availability requires it:

- almond flour;
- tigernut flour;
- coconut flour/milk;
- butter instead of oil;
- maple syrup instead of honey;
- active yeast.

Changing several of these at once would make the result harder to diagnose.

## Kitchen validation matrix

Report these after the loaf is completely cool.

1. **Lift / density** — PASS if clearly lighter than the previous gluten-free loaf and not collapsed or wet-lined in the center.
2. **Crumb / flexibility** — PASS if a normal sandwich slice holds together and bends without behaving like cake crumbs or rubber.
3. **Moisture** — PASS if the center is soft but not gummy, sticky, or obviously under-set; target leans slightly dry rather than moist.
4. **Sandwich + toast** — PASS if it can be sliced at roughly sandwich thickness, handled, and toasted without breaking apart.
5. **Flavor + crust** — PASS if mostly neutral, not strongly flax/cassava flavored, and the crust is acceptably soft.

Also record:

- whether batter needed extra milk or flour;
- approximate finished loaf height;
- any top collapse;
- whether the bottom/sides were tougher than the top;
- how it slices the next morning.

## Next decision

Do not add yeast or specialty binders automatically.

After V1 evidence:

- if crumb is still too dense but structurally good, increase the light starch/oat fraction and/or tune leavening/hydration;
- if crumb is light but fragile, increase binding before adding more dense flour;
- if gummy, reduce hydration and/or cassava before adding more baking time blindly;
- if dry/crumbly, increase hydration/fat/binding modestly;
- if V1 proves quick-bread chemistry itself is the ceiling on lift, then evaluate a controlled single-rise yeast V2.
