# Recipe 0001 — Oatmeal Sandwich Bread

**Status:** Canonical — successful and repeatable  
**Canonical formula source:** Reconstruction V3, first successful documented bake on 2026-09-28  
**Known improvement opportunity:** loaf height / scale

Canonical means this is the current recipe Jim would willingly repeat and recommend. It does **not** mean the recipe is finished forever. The first documented V3 bake hit the requested moisture, flexibility, density, and flavor targets; its shorter loaf height is an improvement opportunity, not a failure.

## What this bread is

A wheat-free Breadman sandwich loaf built around oat flour, arrowroot, cassava, cornstarch, flax, eggs, and yeast.

Target result:

- sandwich and toast friendly;
- soft and light relative to earlier GF attempts;
- between moist and dry, leaning slightly dry;
- flexible rather than crumbly;
- neutral/savory;
- soft enough crust for household sandwich use.

## Ingredients

All measurements use standard US cups/spoons.

### Dry

- 1 cup finely ground oat flour
- 3/4 cup arrowroot starch/flour
- 1/2 cup cassava flour
- 6 Tbsp cornstarch
- 3 Tbsp ground flaxseed meal
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1 tsp fine salt
- 2 tsp active dry yeast

### Wet

- 2 large eggs
- 3/4 cup + 2 Tbsp milk
- 1 Tbsp + 1 tsp honey
- 2 Tbsp olive oil
- 2 tsp apple cider vinegar

### Optional but strongly recommended soft-crust finish

- about 2 Tbsp butter

## Oat flour

If starting from rolled oats, grind them very finely in a blender or food processor, then measure **1 cup of the finished oat flour**.

## Proven Breadman setup

The successful documented bake used:

- **Program:** White
- **Crust:** Light
- **Loaf size:** 1.5 lb

Do not rewrite those settings to 2 lb merely because earlier loaves used 2 lb. The successful V3 evidence belongs to the 1.5 lb White Light run.

## Method

1. Bring the eggs and milk reasonably close to room temperature.
2. Add milk, eggs, honey, olive oil, and apple cider vinegar to the Breadman pan.
3. In a separate bowl, whisk together oat flour, arrowroot, cassava, cornstarch, flax, baking powder, and salt.
4. Add the dry blend over the wet ingredients.
5. Add the active dry yeast according to the Breadman's normal yeast-loading convention.
6. Select **White / Light / 1.5 lb** and start the machine.
7. During the initial mixing stage, scrape the sides/corners if needed so no dry pockets remain.
8. The successful V3 batter was noticeably thick. It was cohesive and held the cavity left by the mixer blade rather than flowing flat.
9. At the **first rest**, remove the mixer blade and gently smooth the batter over with a rubber spatula.
10. Leave the blade out. The Breadman may later perform another spin/punch/rest stage; in the successful run there was therefore no second blade mixing.
11. Let the Breadman complete its remaining rise and bake cycle without adding supplemental oven heat.
12. Remove the loaf from the Breadman pan after baking.
13. For the proven soft-crust finish, melt about **2 Tbsp butter** gently and coat all six sides of the warm loaf. The successful run used 20 seconds at 50% microwave power.
14. Cool in normal loaf orientation on a wire rack until completely cool before slicing.

## Doneness / cooling notes

The successful V3 loaf needed no extra oven time.

A butter-knife check near the end of baking showed no stuck batter, only slight moisture. The final loaf was allowed to cool fully before slicing.

If a future run appears underbaked, record that as a run-specific result rather than silently changing this canonical method.

## Successful V3 result

The documented V3 bake was a **large success** in the requested eating qualities:

- **Moisture:** target hit.
- **Flexibility:** target hit.
- **Density/lightness:** substantially lighter and less dense than earlier GF loaves; target hit.
- **Flavor:** delicious; target hit.
- **Sandwich suitability:** successful.
- **Crust:** sturdy but acceptable after the butter finish.

### Known tradeoff

The loaf rose to roughly **half the height of the earlier GF loaves**.

That is worth improving, but it does not count against this recipe as a successful bread because the eating-quality targets were strong enough to outweigh the smaller scale.

Future experiments should treat **height as an independent improvement target**, not casually change the successful moisture/flexibility/density/flavor balance.
